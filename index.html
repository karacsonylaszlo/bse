01 / THE IDEA

You shouldn't need to know where to stand for a concert to sound good.

Live music should be experienced as the artist intended — whether you're standing at the front, sitting in the middle or watching from the back.

Better Sound Europe is an early-stage grassroots project exploring how measurable technical standards could improve the live audio experience across Europe.

02 / THE PROBLEM

Same concert.
Different experience.

The quality of a live event can vary significantly depending on where you are, how the system is deployed and how the sound is managed.

Explore the issues below.

01 ↗

Uneven Coverage

Large differences in level and frequency response can occur between different areas of the same audience.

WHAT HAPPENS

A concert sound system is expected to cover a large and often irregular audience area. If the system is not properly designed, aligned and measured, people standing only a few metres apart can experience very different sound.

THE EFFECT

One part of the audience may hear a balanced mix while another area may experience excessive bass, insufficient low-end, harsh high frequencies or significantly different overall level.

WHY IT MATTERS

The ticket price is the same regardless of where someone stands. A consistent audience experience therefore requires more than simply having enough loudspeakers.

COVERAGE SYSTEM DESIGN ALIGNMENT
02 SPL

Excessive Exposure

Live events can involve significant sound exposure. Responsible monitoring can help protect audiences.

WHAT HAPPENS

Concerts and festivals can produce high sound pressure levels for extended periods. The actual exposure depends on both the level and the amount of time an audience member is exposed to it.

THE EFFECT

Excessive sound exposure can contribute to temporary hearing fatigue and, with sufficient exposure, may pose a risk to hearing.

WHAT COULD CHANGE

A more consistent approach to measurement and documentation could make sound exposure easier to monitor and communicate to both event operators and audiences.

SPL EXPOSURE SAFETY
03 STI

Poor Clarity

Vocals and important musical elements can become difficult to understand because of system design, acoustics or excessive levels.

WHAT HAPPENS

A live system can be loud while still sounding unclear. Poor acoustic conditions, excessive reverberation, phase interaction, unsuitable system deployment or poor gain structure can all contribute.

THE EFFECT

Vocals may become difficult to understand and individual instruments can lose definition. The audience may hear energy without being able to clearly understand the performance.

OBJECTIVE MEASUREMENT

Where appropriate, objective measurements such as speech intelligibility metrics can help describe clarity rather than relying only on subjective opinions.

INTELLIGIBILITY ACOUSTICS SYSTEM PERFORMANCE
04 EU

No Common Approach

Requirements and practices can vary between countries, venues and event types.

THE CURRENT SITUATION

Live events take place in very different environments — from small clubs and theatres to festivals and large arenas. Technical practices and requirements can therefore differ considerably.

THE PROBLEM

Without a common framework, it can be difficult to define what should be measured, documented and communicated consistently across different events.

THE IDEA

Better Sound Europe is exploring whether common minimum criteria could be developed with input from audio professionals, audiences, event organisers, researchers and other relevant stakeholders.
